Every populated California ZIP code scored for wildfire from the FEMA National Risk Index (December 2025 release, the current edition) at census-tract resolution, weighted to where each ZIP's residents live, and grouped into four tiers. Wildfire is the hazard that decides where a California home policy can be placed, so it is the one this report follows. Your leads come from across California, but 66.7% of the leads in your top eight counties come from Los Angeles County, so this report starts there and then covers the Inland Empire, Orange, San Diego and Sacramento. The good news for your largest market: Los Angeles County is mostly Low tier, with only 9.4% of its housing in High or Extreme ZIPs. The exposure sits one step out, in the Inland Empire, where 56.6% of housing is High or Extreme, and in Ventura County. Section 04 is the recommended treatment for Home leads, section 05 covers Los Angeles County first and then the Inland Empire, Orange, San Diego and Sacramento, and section 06 lays the tiers over your lead counties and cities. The full ZIP-by-ZIP list ships as a spreadsheet with this report.

Scoring system

Public federal data at census-tract resolution, built the way commercial wildfire scores are weighted

What goes into the score
  • Wildfire riskNRI wildfire risk score: burn probability times the building value exposed, scored 0 to 100 nationally.
  • ConcentrationShare of residents in tracts FEMA rates Relatively High or Very High for wildfire. It catches ZIPs where a large score sits in one canyon or hillside tract.
  • ResilienceNRI community resilience: local capacity to recover, a small adjustment.
  • AvailabilityCarrier wildfire scores are property-level (slope, vegetation, access, construction) and not published by ZIP. This composite uses public federal data on the same ingredients.

Tier rules
  • ExtremeResident-weighted NRI wildfire score 95+, or 50%+ of residents in high-risk tracts
  • HighScore 80+, or 20%+ of residents in high-risk tracts
  • ModerateScore 45+, 5%+ of residents in high-risk tracts, or any single tract at 99+
  • LowEverything else.
  • How Extreme is set585 of the 614 Extreme ZIPs reach the tier on the score itself; the other 29 reach it because half or more of their residents live in high-risk tracts, as in Temecula 92592.

04

Home leads: recommended treatment

Extreme tier gets a placement check · High tier gets a construction and defensible-space review

Check appetite · Extreme · 614 ZIPs · 9.5% of CA housing Construction and defensible-space review · High · 287 ZIPs · 14.8% No change · 861 ZIPs · 75.7%
Excluding every Extreme ZIP would remove 9.5% of California housing from Home; adding the High tier would bring it to 24.3%. We do not recommend a blanket exclusion. We recommend excluding only the ZIPs where new home business cannot be written today, and treating the rest of the Extreme and High tiers as a conversation: roof, vents and siding, defensible space around the home, and how the home would be placed if the standard market declines it.
Home treatment by tier
  • ExtremeCheck appetite. Confirm the carrier is writing new home business in the ZIP before the first call. Quote Auto on its own merits, and if the home cannot be placed in the standard market, refer it for placement (the California FAIR Plan plus a difference-in-conditions policy) rather than leading with a bundle discount.
  • HighConstruction and defensible-space review. Ask about the roof material and age, ember-resistant vents, the siding and deck, and the cleared space around the home. Mitigation like this can matter for eligibility and pricing, so ask before promising a bundle.
  • ModerateNo change. Offer the bundle; expect some properties near open land to need an inspection.
  • LowNo change. Offer the home or renters bundle on every Auto quote; standard placement is most likely here.

Your lead regions

Los Angeles County
Los Angeles County holds 288 ZIPs: 22 Extreme, 24 High, 35 Moderate and 207 Low. The basin, from the Westside through central Los Angeles to Long Beach and Whittier, is almost all Low. The exposure follows the hills and the edges: Malibu, the Santa Clarita Valley (Santa Clarita, Canyon Country, Valencia, Newhall), Porter Ranch and Tarzana, the San Gabriel foothills (Altadena, San Dimas, La Verne, Claremont), Diamond Bar, and Palmdale. 07

Wildfire in the conversation

Questions that make a California home quote faster and stickier

Why it matters here
  • Placement7.8% of housing in your lead footprint sits in Extreme ZIPs, including 25.4% in Riverside, 19.8% in Ventura and 17.8% in San Bernardino. Checking whether the home can be written before the first call keeps the conversation honest and the Auto sale intact.
  • The home itselfRoof material and age, ember-resistant vents, siding, decks and the cleared space around the home all matter for wildfire. Asking early shows the customer you know the market and speeds up any inspection.
  • If the home is declinedThe California FAIR Plan provides basic fire coverage, and a difference-in-conditions policy fills the gaps. Explaining that path, and keeping the Auto policy, keeps the household with you.
  • EarthquakeEarthquake is excluded from a standard home policy and written separately. 71% of footprint residents live in tracts FEMA rates high for earthquake, including 93% in Los Angeles County, so an earthquake quote is a natural follow-up on Low-wildfire homes too.
  • AutoFire damage to a car is paid under comprehensive coverage. On every Auto quote in a High or Extreme ZIP, confirm comprehensive is included; it is also the natural opening to quote the home.
